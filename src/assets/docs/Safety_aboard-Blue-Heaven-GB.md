# Safety onboard Blue Heaven

## Blue Heaven

*A description of the boat's safety equipment, and the principles we sail by*

**Boat:** Blue Heaven  **Skippers:** Louise Juhl / Philip Juhl

## 1. Purpose and responsibility

Safety onboard Blue Heaven has the highest priority. It matters to us that everyone onboard feels safe and knows how we handle the situations that can arise on a longer passage.

The skippers have overall responsibility for the boat, navigation and safety onboard. The skippers make the final decisions about sailing, weather, safety procedures and any changes to the plans.

Everyone onboard must follow the agreed safety rules, point out potential hazards, and speak up if they feel unsafe or notice something that needs attention.

This document describes the boat's safety equipment, our safety procedures and the basic principles we follow during offshore sailing.

## 2. The boat's safety equipment

Blue Heaven carries the following safety equipment:

**Rescue and personal safety equipment**
- 6-person life raft.
- Offshore lifejackets for everyone onboard.
- Safety tethers and jacklines on deck.
- Lifebuoy.

**Navigation and communication**
- Garmin inReach satellite communication device.
- Fixed VHF with DSC and GPS.
- Handheld VHF radio.
- Radar.
- AIS with both transmit and receive.
- Chartplotter, and Navionics charts on an iPad.

**Fire and gas protection**
- CO₂ fire extinguisher.
- Powder extinguishers in the cockpit and in every cabin.
- Smoke alarms.
- Gas alarm.

**Emergency and rescue equipment**
- Windvane, which can also be used as an emergency rudder if there is a problem with the primary steering system.
- Wire cutters and a battery-powered angle grinder, so the rig can be freed if the mast or the standing rigging fails.
- Searchlight.
- Foghorn.
- Medicine chest.

**Bilge equipment**
- Two electric bilge pumps.
- One manual bilge pump.

## 3. Safety briefing before an offshore passage

Before we start an offshore passage, we go through the boat's safety equipment and procedures with everyone onboard.

The briefing covers, among other things:

- **Man overboard (MOB):** How we respond if a person falls overboard, including raising the alarm, manoeuvring and using the rescue equipment.
- **Safety equipment:** Where the equipment is, how it is used, and where the relevant switches and controls are.
- **Abandon ship:** When and how we leave the boat, where the life raft is, and which equipment to take.
- **Fire and gas:** How we respond to a fire or a gas leak, and where the firefighting equipment is.
- **Communication:** How we communicate onboard and with the outside world in an emergency.

## 4. Safety during an offshore passage

We follow a few fixed principles to reduce the risk of accidents while sailing.

**Lifejackets and tethers**
- A lifejacket is always worn in the cockpit or on deck during an offshore passage.
- A tether is used when you are on deck.
- No one goes on deck unless another person is in the cockpit.
- Tethers are clipped to the boat's jacklines or to approved strong points.

**Sailing at night**
- We reduce the sail area at night and sail with less sail, so the boat is easier to handle.
- We never sail with a spinnaker or a gennaker at night.
- We pay particular attention to every movement on deck, and the safety procedures are always followed.

**Alcohol and other intoxicants**
- We do not drink alcohol or take other intoxicants while sailing.

**Weather and sailing conditions**
- We adapt the sailing to the weather and reduce sail in good time when conditions call for it.
- We always put safety before speed and progress.
- The skippers keep checking the forecasts and the conditions, and decide whether to continue or change the passage.

## 5. Maintenance and checks of the safety equipment

We make sure the boat's equipment is maintained and works as it should.

The following is part of our regular maintenance:

- Lifejackets are inspected and serviced every year.
- The life raft is serviced according to the manufacturer's instructions and the required service intervals.
- The rig was inspected in 2025 and is inspected every year.
- The rudder bearings were replaced in 2026.
- The engine and saildrive are serviced every year.
- The rest of the boat's safety equipment is checked regularly.

We also check the boat's essential systems and equipment before longer offshore passages.

---

**Our basic principle is simple:**

We sail to have good experiences and to enjoy life at sea. It should feel safe to be onboard, and we never compromise on safety in order to reach a destination or keep to a schedule.
