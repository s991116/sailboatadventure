# 1\. Purpose and role

The agreement is meant to set clear expectations between the skippers and the crew about the voyage, life onboard, finances and safety.

The crew takes part by sailing and helping onboard, and not as a paying passenger. The crew should therefore, to the best of their ability, take an active part in the sailing and the ordinary tasks onboard.

# 2\. The skippers’ responsibility and authority

The skippers have overall responsibility for the boat, navigation and safety onboard. Everyone onboard must follow the decisions of the skipper on watch about sailing and safety.

# 3\. Safety

On joining, the skippers go through the relevant safety matters, including:

* life jackets, lifelines and safe movement on deck  
* man-overboard procedure  
* fire, firefighting equipment and gas  
* VHF and other emergency communication  
* life raft, EPIRB and abandon-ship procedure  
* emergency exits, electricity and other relevant procedures.

The crew must use the safety equipment as instructed and speak up at once if there is any doubt, illness, exhaustion or any condition that may affect safety.

# 4\. Passage plan

The stated route and schedule are indicative. The skippers may change the route, departure time, destination or harbour because of, among other things:

* weather, wind and conditions at sea  
* technical problems  
* the crew’s safety or health  
* harbour, authority or border conditions  
* other circumstances that the skippers consider important for a safe passage.

The crew accepts that exact departure and arrival times cannot be guaranteed. The crew should therefore plan onward travel with suitable flexibility in timing.

# 5\. The crew’s participation

The crew is expected, according to ability and experience, to take part in:

* helm and night watches  
* sail handling  
* mooring and anchoring  
* cooking and washing up  
* cleaning and tidying  
* provisioning  
* other ordinary practical tasks onboard.

A lack of sailing experience is not necessarily a problem. The crew is expected to be willing to learn, ask when in doubt, and follow instructions.

# 6\. Watches

On longer stretches the crew takes part in the boat’s watch system. The skippers set the watch rota based on the conditions, the size of the crew and their experience. Anything that may limit the crew’s participation in watches must be stated before joining.

# 7\. Life onboard

Blue Heaven is both a sailing yacht and a home during the voyage. Everyone onboard is therefore expected to:

* show consideration and help keep a good atmosphere  
* keep their own belongings in order and travel light  
* help with cleaning and shared tasks  
* limit the use of water, power and gas  
* respect the need for privacy, sleep and rest  
* raise problems or discomfort early and respectfully.

# 8\. Finances

**Crew contribution:** DKK 250 per day

**Payment terms:**

* 50% of the total crew contribution for the passage is paid no later than 1 month before the agreed joining date.  
* The remaining 50% is paid ongoing as agreed between the skippers and the crew.

The crew contribution covers maintenance of the boat’s equipment and servicing of, for example, the engine, sails and the boat’s gear, and is not payment for a commercial service.

**The crew contribution does not include:**

* Restaurant visits  
* Personal purchases  
* Transport to and from the boat, as well as visas, entry fees or similar

Costs that are not described as included are, as a rule, paid by the crew.

# 9\. Cancellation, changes and ending early

**Cancellation by the crew before joining**

If the crew chooses to cancel before joining, 50% of the total agreed crew contribution is refunded. The remaining 50% is not repaid.

**Cancellation or changes by the skippers before joining**

If the skippers cancel the passage entirely before joining, the full amount paid is repaid to the crew.

If the passage is delayed or changed substantially, the parties agree a fair handling of the crew contribution already paid, taking into account the scale of the delay or change and any costs already incurred.

**Ending after joining**

If the cooperation is not working, or if safety requires it, both the crew and the skippers may end the agreement at the next suitable and safe harbour. Payment for the remainder of the passage is returned to the crew.

# 10\. Insurance, documents and authority requirements

The crew is responsible for having:

* a valid passport and any necessary visas or entry permits  
* relevant health cover  
* their own travel insurance that is valid for the whole period  
* insurance cover for the specific type of sailing, including offshore or long-distance sailing where relevant.

The crew must check the insurance terms themselves.

# 11\. Health and medicine

Before joining, the crew must tell the skippers about health conditions, allergies, dietary needs, functional limitations or medicine that may be relevant to safety or life onboard. Safety or life onboard. The crew must also say who should be contacted in an emergency.

# 12\. Alcohol, smoking and intoxicants

Alcohol is only consumed by agreement with the skippers, and never in connection with watches, while under way, or in situations where safety may be affected.

Smoking is only allowed outside, in the cockpit or on deck, as directed by the skippers. Illegal or intoxicating drugs are not accepted onboard.

# 13\. Personal belongings

Personal belongings are brought and stored at the crew’s own risk. Luggage should be kept limited and carried in soft bags that can be stowed away.
